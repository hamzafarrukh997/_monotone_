> **TL;DR:** You don't have to choose. A website, a Facebook page, and an Instagram account do three different jobs, and a local business needs all three working as one system. The difference that matters isn't which one is "best." It's that you own your website and only rent your social pages. Facebook and Instagram bring people in. Your website is where they decide to trust you and buy. If a rented page disappears overnight, the audience goes with it. What you own, you keep.

## Do you need a website if you already have a Facebook page?

Yes, because they aren't the same tool doing the same job. A Facebook page is where people find you. A website is where people decide about you.

Watch how a customer actually moves. They see your post while scrolling. They check your Instagram to see if you look real. Then, if they're about to spend money, they look for a proper website to confirm you're a legitimate business and not a page that will vanish next week. Each channel catches them at a different moment. Remove one and there's a gap in the journey.

So the real question isn't "website or Facebook." It's what each one does, and what happens if you skip one.

## What's the actual difference between a website, a Facebook page, and an Instagram account?

The difference is ownership. You own your website. You rent your Facebook page and your Instagram account, and the landlord can change the rules or evict you without warning.

That one fact changes how a serious business should treat all three:

A website is property you own. You control it, nobody else can switch it off, and it's the one place a customer can always find the same complete information about you.

A Facebook page is rented space. The reach is real, but Facebook decides who sees your posts, and it has cut that reach before. Businesses lost significant traffic when Facebook changed its algorithm in 2023, overnight, through no fault of their own.

An Instagram account is a rented shop window. Good for showing your work, weak at holding detailed information, and tied to the same platform risk as Facebook.

Rented space isn't bad. Most of your customers live there. Pakistan has around 80 million active social media users, and Facebook alone reaches roughly 69 million people. Ignoring that would be a mistake. But renting with nothing you own to send people to means your whole business sits on ground someone else controls.

## What is each channel actually for?

Each channel has one main job. Trying to make one channel do all three is where local businesses waste money and lose customers.

### Facebook page: getting found and staying reachable

Facebook is your reach layer. It's where the most local customers already are, where they message you, and where reviews and word of mouth spread. Its job is to put you in front of people and let them contact you easily. Its weakness is depth. Nobody reads a full service list or price breakdown inside a Facebook page, and you don't control who sees your posts.

### Instagram account: proving you're real and showing your work

Instagram is your proof layer. For a visual business, such as a salon, a bakery, a workshop, or a clothing brand, it's where a stranger checks whether your work looks good and whether you're an active, real operation. Its job is to build quick visual trust. Its weakness is that it holds almost no usable information: no proper way to explain services, answer questions, or take a structured enquiry.

### Website: converting interest into a customer

Your website is your conversion layer. It's the only channel where you control the whole experience: complete information, clear pricing or enquiry paths, proof, guarantees, and a way to contact you that doesn't depend on a platform's algorithm. Its job is to turn "this looks interesting" into "I'll book them." It's also the one channel machines like Google and AI assistants can read and recommend. Your social pages are mostly invisible to them.

## Why can't I just use social media and skip the website?

Because the moment a customer is about to spend real money, they look for the thing you own. If it isn't there, doubt fills the gap.

Your local buyer has been scammed before, or knows someone who has. A business with only a Facebook page reads, to them, as a business that could disappear. A website, even a simple one, signals permanence: a real operation that has invested in being findable and isn't going anywhere.

There's a practical trap too. If your only presence is rented and the platform suspends your page, changes its reach rules, or you lose access to the account, your audience doesn't come with you. You start from zero. Anything built on rented ground can be taken. Anything you own, you keep.

## So what does a local business actually need?

All three, in the right order, working as one system, not three things you post to at random.

The order matters. Build the website first, as the foundation: it's the one asset you own and the place every other channel points to, and without it your social effort leaks customers at the final step. Use Facebook to be found and reachable, since it's the widest net for local customers and the easiest place for them to message you. Use Instagram to prove you're real, because it's the visual trust builder that turns a curious scroller into someone willing to click through to your site.

Set up this way, the three stop competing and start feeding each other. Social brings the traffic, the website converts it, and the audience you build sits partly on ground you actually own. That's the difference between a presence someone else can wipe out and one they can't.
