Most teams reach for cleverness when what their visitors actually want is
clarity. A confident, plainly-worded page that answers the obvious questions
will almost always out-convert a beautiful one that makes people work.

## Trust is built in the first few seconds

Before anyone reads your carefully-crafted paragraph, they've already made a
snap judgement. A few things move that judgement in your favour:

- **Say what you do** in the first line, in words a normal person uses.
- **Show proof** — real work, real outcomes, real names.
- **Remove friction** — one clear next step, not five competing ones.

## Cleverness is a tax on comprehension

Every pun, every abstract hero headline, every "reimagining the future of"
sentence asks the reader to decode before they can decide. Some brands can
afford that tax. Most can't.

> Clarity isn't the absence of personality. It's personality that respects the
> reader's time.

## Where this shows up in our work

This principle threads through everything from the first wireframe to the final
piece of copy — which is exactly why design and growth work best together
rather than in sequence.
