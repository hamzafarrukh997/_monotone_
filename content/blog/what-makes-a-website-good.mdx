> **TL;DR:** A good website is not the prettiest one. It's the one that loads fast, works properly on a phone, makes your offer clear in seconds, gives strangers a reason to trust you, and makes contacting you easy. Looks matter for the first impression, but the job of a good site is to turn a visitor into an enquiry. Use the plain-English checklist below to judge any website, including your own, without needing to be a designer.

Most people judge a website by whether it looks nice. That's the wrong test, because a beautiful site that loads slowly, confuses the visitor, or hides the contact button is a bad website with good paint. Here's a checklist you can actually use.

## What makes a website "good"?

A good website is one that turns visitors into enquiries, because it loads fast, is clear, works on mobile, earns trust, and makes it easy to act. That's the whole definition. Everything else serves those five jobs.

Notice what's not in that definition: awards, animations, or being the most stylish site in your industry. Those can help, but a site can have all of them and still fail the only test that matters, which is whether a real customer contacts you after visiting.

## Does a good website just mean good design?

No. Design decides the first impression, but function decides whether the visit becomes a customer. You need both, and most weak websites have the look without the function.

Design matters more than people think at the very start: visitors form a first impression of a website in about 50 milliseconds (Lindgaard et al., 2006). That split second decides whether they stay. But staying is not the same as acting. After that first impression, speed, clarity, trust, and an easy way to contact you are what turn a visitor into an enquiry. A good website wins both moments, not just the first.

## The plain-English checklist for a good website

A good website passes these eight checks. You don't need to be technical to test any of them. Go through them on your own site, on your phone.

**It loads fast.** The site should be usable in under three seconds on a normal phone connection. 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes more than 3 seconds to load (Google, 2017). Speed is not a luxury, it's the entry ticket.

**It works on a phone.** Text is readable without pinching, buttons are easy to tap, and nothing is cut off. Most of your visitors are on phones, so a site that only looks right on a laptop is failing most of its audience.

**It says what you do in seconds.** On the first screen, before any scrolling, a stranger should understand what you offer and who it's for. Clever taglines that hide the point cost you customers.

**It has one obvious next step.** Every page makes it clear what to do next: message on WhatsApp, call, or fill a short form. The action is visible without hunting, and repeated as the page goes on.

**It gives reasons to trust you.** Real photos of your work, genuine reviews, faces of the team, and proof of past jobs. A cautious stranger needs evidence before they'll risk an enquiry.

**The content is written for customers, not filler.** The words speak to what your customer worries about and wants, in plain language. Generic, machine-filler text that could belong to any business quietly signals that nobody cared.

**It's findable.** The site has the basics in place so it can show up when people search for what you do. A site nobody can find is a brochure in a drawer.

**It's secure and yours.** The address starts with https, and you own the domain, the hosting, and the files. If someone else controls those, you're renting your own website.

## How do I check if my website is actually good?

Run the checklist on your phone as if you were a first-time customer, and count where you hesitate. Every hesitation is a place a real visitor would leave.

Time the load. Read the first screen and ask if a stranger would understand it. Try to contact yourself and count the taps. Look for anything that would make a cautious person trust you. If you get stuck or annoyed at any step, that's not a small flaw, it's the exact point where enquiries are being lost.

A good website is quietly effective, not just attractive. If yours passes these checks, the design is doing real work. If it fails two or three, that's your fix list, in order.
